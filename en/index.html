Does Meta take down more left-wing accounts?

On one side, left-wing profiles banned without explanation and more than a thousand reports of restrictions on Lula supporters. On the other, Nikolas Ferreira with more engagement than any Brazilian celebrity. Meta denies favoring anyone. Here is the comparison, side by side, with the same standard.

📊 See the summary in icons🎮 Play: Moderator for a Day

The short answer

It cannot be proven yet, but the signs point more to one side. Large left-wing accounts were banned by Meta itself, and one organization gathered 1,121 reports of restrictions on Lula supporters during the campaign. On the right, we found few cases and no similar survey. Nikolas Ferreira's engagement is off the charts, but that alone does not prove a push from Meta. The key piece is missing: Meta's own data, which the TSE (Brazil's electoral court) requested and the company has not yet fully delivered.

Four ways an account can disappear

Not every account that disappears was Meta's choice. Before comparing cases, we need to know who decided:

⚖️Court orderA judge orders it taken down. The company only complies.
📕Platform ruleMeta says the account broke its “community standards”.
🛠️Error or glitchA bot or a bug takes the account down, and later it comes back.
🔇Less reachThe account stays up, but far fewer people see its posts.
Who decides: the courtsWho decides: Meta itself

This route covers the last three: what Meta does on its own. When the order comes from the courts, the question is a different one, and it is on the Washington Route.

What about the “Exército do Lula” channel?

It went down, but by court order, not by Meta's decision. Meta only complied. That is why it is not counted among the bans on this route.

What happened, step by step

  • October 11, morning. At the request of Flávio's campaign, the president of the TSE, Nunes Marques, suspends the WhatsApp channel of deputy André Janones, which has about 3.4 million followers. He said rallying supporters is allowed, but that the channel was spreading “facts known to be untrue” against a candidate in an organized way.
  • October 11, afternoon. The TSE's inspector general, Cueva, puts the channel back online until the court's full bench rules on the case.
  • October 13. Nunes Marques annuls Cueva's decision. The channel stays offline until Lula's campaign registers its address with the Electoral Courts, with a 48-hour waiting period. In the latest news, the channel was offline.
  • What changes for Meta. It must watch the channel and delete copies of posts the courts have already ordered removed. The same rule applies to public channels linked to Flávio's campaign, “with objective and equal criteria”.

The 1,121 reports

CTRL+Z, an organization that holds big tech accountable, opened a form for anyone whose pro-Lula profile or post was restricted on Meta during the campaign. As of October 13, there were 1,121 reports. They break down like this:

In numbers: about 412 are about reduced reach, 359 about blocked features and 247 about removed posts or accounts. Of these, 108 are entire accounts banned. The reports include Lula's official profile, with 16.9 million followers.

What the survey shows

  • Many people on the same side reporting the same kind of problem, in the same period.
  • Most of it is about reach and features, not bans.

What it does not show

  • These are reports sent by the people themselves. CTRL+Z has not explained how it checks each one.
  • There is no equivalent survey for right-wing profiles, so no comparison is possible.

Meta responded that there were “technical failures” in posts and ads from Lula's campaign, but that the accusation of favoring Flávio “is false, it has not been demonstrated”.

Why it looks suspicious

  • The Pix video, in January 2025, got about 300 million views in two days. Brazil has about 130 million Instagram accounts.
  • A week earlier, Meta had started recommending political content in the feed again, to people who do not follow the profile. It did not confirm whether the change was already in effect in Brazil.
  • A company in which Nikolas is a partner spent more than R$745,000 on Meta ads between April 2024 and April 2025.
  • His performance is much higher on Instagram, owned by Meta, than on TikTok, YouTube or X.

Why it is not proof

  • A view is not a person: the same person counts several times, and there are viewers outside Brazil.
  • Aos Fatos found no sign of paid boosting on the Pix video.
  • The recommendation of political content applies to all profiles, not only right-wing ones. A profile that already has a lot of engagement gains even more from it.
  • Each network has its own audience and its own way of counting interactions. Many politicians do better on one network than on the others.

What would prove a push from Meta: how many of Nikolas's views come from people who do not follow him, compared with left-wing profiles of the same size. Only Meta has that data.

Why a list does not prove persecution

A list of cases shows that something happened. It does not show whether it happens more to one side than the other. Think of it like this:

📰We only see what makes the news

People with a lawyer, a friendly congressman or many followers can get into the paper. Small accounts that go down, on either side, almost never do.

🧮The total is missing

To say “more”, we need to know how many left-wing and how many right-wing accounts were taken down in the same period, under the same rules. Only Meta has that number.

🔍The reason is missing

Meta almost never says which post broke which rule. Without that, nobody can check whether the rule was the same for everyone.

This cuts both ways: the lack of data does not prove there is persecution, and it does not prove there is none.

What Meta says and what the TSE asked for

Meta's accusers say

  • Lula's campaign speaks of a “pattern” of reach drops, blocked ads and no explanations.
  • A PT report says Flávio's page has more engagement than Lula's, despite having fewer followers.
  • Influencers who were taken down say they do not know which rule they broke and cannot appeal on the platform itself.

Meta says

  • The accusations are “simply false”, and the rules apply equally to everyone.
  • The same recommendation limit applies to Lula's and Flávio's profiles.
  • Some of the cases were worldwide glitches. The block on Lula's ads was an error by an anti-scam filter that became stricter after an agreement with the Attorney General's Office (AGU) in April 2026.

What the TSE asked for on October 10

Justice Bellizze gave Meta 48 hours to explain reach, recommendations, blocks and ads. On October 12 Meta said it had complied fully, but these were still missing:

  • the list of restrictions applied to Lula's profiles since August 16;
  • the dates on which the filters were switched on for each candidacy;
  • the reach numbers that would prove the videos kept being delivered;
  • whether Flávio's ads went through the same checks.

These are exactly the data that would allow the two sides to be compared.

Is it true?

True

“Meta took down Jones Manoel's accounts.” His Instagram and Facebook went offline on August 6, 2025 and came back two days later, with no explanation either time.

Half true

“Meta banned more than a thousand accounts of Lula supporters.” These are 1,121 reports of several kinds of restriction, most of them about reach and features. Banned accounts number 108. The reports were not verified one by one.

True

“Meta blocked the Lula campaign's ads.” Meta itself admitted to the TSE that it deactivated the ad account by mistake on September 23, 2026. It says the account came back in about an hour.

False

“Lula was banned from Instagram.” In December 2025 the profiles of Lula and PT members disappeared from search for a few hours. The account was not banned.

True

“Nikolas has more engagement than Brazilian celebrities.” In April 2026, his average per post beat Virgínia Fonseca's and Bruna Marquezine's.

True

“Nikolas gets more engagement than Kim Kardashian.” With 15 times fewer followers, he gets about 4 times more interactions per post, and an engagement rate about 65 times higher. The measurements come from different tools, but the gap is too large to be only that.

True

"Nikolas gets far more engagement on Instagram than on other networks." From February to April 2026, each Instagram post got about 1.33 million interactions, against 145,000 on TikTok and 64,000 on YouTube. This does not prove a push from Meta: each network counts differently and has a different audience.

Half true

“Nikolas's video had more views than there are Instagram users in Brazil.” The numbers add up: about 300 million views and about 130 million accounts. But the same person counts several times and there are viewers abroad, so this alone does not prove fraud or a push.

False

“Only left-wing accounts get taken down.” Meta also took down a network linked to the Bolsonaro family's offices in 2020, and Nikolas's posts disappeared in 2026. But in the cases we found, the left shows up far more often.

Open

“Meta persecutes the left.” There are many cases and more than a thousand reports, but no Meta data comparing the two sides. The TSE asked for it, and Meta has not delivered all of it yet.

Not shown

“Meta boosts Nikolas and the right.” The engagement is off the charts, but we found no data showing Meta pushing it. To know, one would need to see how many of the views come from people who do not follow the profile.

In short

  • On the left, large accounts were banned by Meta itself, almost always without saying which rule was broken. There are also 1,121 reports of restrictions on Lula supporters, not yet verified.
  • On the right, we found few cases and no similar survey. The biggest was the disappearance of Nikolas's posts, still unexplained.
  • Nikolas's engagement is the highest on Brazilian Instagram and beats Kim Kardashian's, Beyoncé's and Kylie Jenner's per post. That raises suspicion, but does not prove a push from Meta.
  • To complete the comparison, the data that only Meta has is missing: how many accounts on each side were restricted and where the views come from. The TSE has asked for it.

A case with no political side

On October 17 and 18, 2025, Meta took down about 50 cannabis-related profiles, such as the Marcha da Maconha and patients' associations. At least 20 came back two days later, and the company admitted to the government that it had made a mistake. The case shows that Meta's filters also make mistakes with no political motive.

The link with Washington

In January 2025, Mark Zuckerberg announced the end of third-party fact-checking in the US and said he would work with Trump against governments that “censor” American companies, citing courts in Latin America. That does not prove Meta acted on the Trump administration's orders in Brazil. That part of the story is on the Washington Route.

Sources and limits of this reading

The list gathers cases reported in the news with a name and a date, and is not a complete count. CTRL+Z's 1,121 reports are self-reported, and there is no equivalent survey for the right. Engagement rankings come from private companies, with their own methods. Cases involving people with less visibility are left out, on both sides. A drop in reach, a loss of followers or a restored account, on their own, do not show political intent. That requires comparing accounts, periods, rules applied and technical records. Meta's denial does not replace that verification either. Each account's status is as of the latest news we found.
